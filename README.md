# pygeoinf0

Continuum-first inference. Sets, measures, function spaces (L2, Lp, Sobolev) and operators are mathematical objects;
their numerics are explicit, replaceable realisations (Legendre series, tensor products, spherical harmonics, Matern
kernels), so a space has no basis and no dimension, and a minimum-norm or Bayesian solution lives exactly in the span
of the data kernels.

Released under the BSD 3-Clause licence (LICENSE). Inspired by pygeoinf (D. Al-Attar, BSD-3-Clause), whose ideas it
builds on while taking different design choices. This is a snapshot release without development history; mentions
below of plans, experiments (`experiments/EXP-...`), AGENTS.md or `docs/agent-docs` refer to the author's development
records, which are not included. Plugins mentioned below are separate packages and are not part of this release.

```bash
pip install -e ".[plot]"          # numpy, scipy; matplotlib for plots
python -m pytest -q               # the fast suite (Testing, below)
```

Examples: `examples/global_tomography.py` (whole-Earth linearised P tomography with SensRay rays in PREM, 10,000 travel
times, one file), `examples/tomography.ipynb` (rays in 2-D, 3-D and on the sphere; generated by
`examples/make_tomography_notebook.py`). Large Gram matrices and evaluations run in blocks over forked worker processes
when `PYGEOINF0_WORKERS=n` is set (Linux; one BLAS thread per worker).

## Testing
Run from src/. A test marked `@pytest.mark.slow(reason="...")` is skipped unless `PYGEOINF0_SLOW_TESTS=1`, and its
skip reason says so (the marker lives in `tests/conftest.py`). The slow tests are the weighted-measure routes of
EXP-031 that take tens of seconds to minutes each: the six Kuhn tetrahedra, joins of simplices, the weighted shell
(s = 2), 3-D boxes, split boxes, four triangles, and the corrected boxes and curves of the spectral engine.

```bash
python -m pytest -q                              # fast suite: 851 passed, 10 skipped
PYGEOINF0_SLOW_TESTS=1 python -m pytest -q       # full suite: 860 passed, 1 skipped
```

On europa (OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=4, 2026-10-28, while a 24-process export kept the load at about 26) the fast suite
took 350 s and the full suite 1093 s; the Kuhn tetrahedra alone take about 6 minutes. The one remaining skip needs
worker processes (`PYGEOINF0_WORKERS=2..4`).

Law tests (plan 023, from EXP-036): `tests/test_laws.py` checks algebraic laws (adjoints under metrics, of
compositions and sums; symmetric inner products; finite-rank covariances; the posterior against the information form;
claims never stronger than their premises; Kleene logic; bracket scaling; a covariance's claims true of it and its trace
enclosed) with Hypothesis on generated valid objects
and rounding-error tolerances. It needs the optional `hypothesis` (in the `dev` extra) and is skipped without it.
Every law has a planted-defect control, a test that monkeypatches a known defect in and requires the law to fail: a
law whose control passes checks nothing. A new law goes into `LAWS` with its control (`test_every_law_has_a_control`).

## Design decisions (and where the mathematics corrected the wish list)
1. **Endpoints.** `Interval` keeps open/closed ends (membership, and later C(I) and Sobolev traces), but L2 on [0, 1] and on (0, 1) is one space: endpoints are a null set. `L2("[0,1]") == L2("(0,1)")`.
2. **Measure space.** A measure carries its set and sigma-algebra, so `LebesgueMeasure(I)` *is* (I, Sigma, mu); no separate MeasureSpace class is needed. `mu(Interval(...))` measures sets. `L2(I)` (an interval instead of a measure) is a shortcut for `L2(LebesgueMeasure(I))`, the convention behind writing L2(0, 1); the default is printed (`L2([0, 1], Lebesgue)`) and recorded in `M.claims`.
3. **Elements are classes.** `f(x)` is the canonical representative (continuous where the class has one; mean of one-sided limits at a jump), which depends only on the class; `f.rep(x)` is the representative the user gave. `f == g` means equal almost everywhere (to the realisation tolerance).
4. **Plotting a class.** Literally impossible (a plot samples finitely many points, a null set). The package draws the canonical representative, cell averages (invariant under null-set changes) and, on request, a reminder of other representatives.
5. **Realisation is explicit.** Elements are realised adaptively as piecewise Legendre series to about 1e-14 with an L2 error estimate. Spaces have no basis and no dimension. All arithmetic and inner products are exact on the realisations; jumps are located by bisection to adjacent floats. A callable is known only where it is sampled, so every fit from one states its **resolution** in its claims (twice the largest spacing of its samples: 0.000977 on [0, 1] by default); a narrower feature may be lost without a warning. `M(f, resolution=w)` declares the narrowest feature width w, the fit samples at w/2 and its claims state w met (cost about (2 L / w)^d samples); `breaks=` and `support=` remain. During a warning period a fit with none of the three gives a `ResolutionWarning`; `pg.settings.resolution_policy = "refuse"` refuses it now, the later default.
6. **Point evaluation is refused on L2** (not defined on classes, not bounded); local averages are offered.
7. **Products.** L2 is not closed under products in general; products of realised elements are allowed because realisations are bounded; NumPy functions compose with the representative and are refitted.
8. **Random elements** need a measure: L2 has no standard Gaussian (white noise is not in L2), and no space picks a prior for the caller (plan 069 P6, decision Q2): `X.gaussian()` and `M.random()` on L2 refuse with `pg.PriorUnstated`, naming the constructors. Priors are named: `pygeoinf0.probability.legendre_decay(M, decay)`, `random_features(M, length_scale, features, seed)` (its approximation of the squared-exponential covariance claimed), `cylindrical(H, alpha)` (= `pg.CylindricalGaussian(H, alpha)` in infinite dimension); `D.standard_gaussian()` and `D.random` use N(0, W^-1), the standard Gaussian of a finite-dimensional inner product.
9. **Weighted R^n** is L2 of a weighted counting measure; a general SPD metric is allowed but is not a measure.
10. **Operators.** `Functionals(kernels)` is the stack of integrals m -> (<k_i, m>)_i; its adjoint is y -> sum (W y)_i k_i; `G @ G.adjoint` becomes the Gram matrix with derived claims (self-adjoint, PSD). The minimum-norm solution lives in the span of the kernels, so it is finite-dimensional exactly: no truncation of M is needed.
11. **Solvers.** `A.inv` is the inverse, computed to a stated precision, or refused with the choices named; `A.pseudo_inverse(rcond)` is the pseudo-inverse with a threshold you choose. Below a condition of 1e10 it is Cholesky; above that the spectrum is checked and nothing is dropped; an eigenvalue at most eps n times its block's largest has no correct digit, and the result is refused. `.claims` and `.report()` state the condition and the digits lost; `pg.errors.PrecisionWarning` warns from 1e10. Those digits count rounding only: an error in the operator itself, as in tabulated kernels, comes on top. `pg.solvers.usage()` gives each solver's object, when to use it, the error it can deliver, its cost and any simplification, for people and agents.
12. **Speed.** Everything heavy is a BLAS call on coefficient arrays. Results of `G(x)` are remembered on x (elements are immutable and their arrays read-only). No compiled code is needed at these sizes.


## Known limits
Sparse families with a density measure; arithmetic on sparse families; unbounded intervals; Sobolev spaces of negative
order as spaces (dual functionals are measures);
norms of formulas in restriction Sobolev spaces (`NormUnavailable`); exact zonal kernels for arcs on the sphere. A formula
given to H^s (s >= 1/2) is refused when its realisation jumps at a break, and for s >= m + 1/2 when its m-th
derivative does (`NotAnElement`, plan 065), on intervals and paths only: on 2-D and 3-D domains its membership is
ASSERTED in its claims and not checked; a bank function's regularity gives the theorem on every domain. Narrow
features between samples can be missed (stated in the claims; pass `resolution=` or `breaks=`). Realising very large families costs about 10-50
microseconds per function. Distances between elements realised on different families (say a Kronecker and a dense
family of the same kernels) come from a quadratic form over both families and resolve differences only to about
sqrt(eps) = 1.5e-8 relative; compare values, or realise both on one family. Later sections list the limits of each round.


## Which spaces exist on which domains?

```python
import pygeoinf0 as pg
pg.catalogue.table()                       # domains x spaces: ok, c (constructed only), no (refused), - (not applicable)
pg.catalogue.plot()                        # the same as a coloured grid (matplotlib)
pg.catalogue.how("Box", "Spec Mixed")      # one entry: the commands in order, its status, for a refusal the reason
pg.catalogue.how("Box")                    # a row: every space on that domain
pg.catalogue.how(space="Spec Mixed")       # a column: every domain for that space
```

A domain comes first and a space is built on it. `how` prints exactly that, numbered, and the recipe runs as written:

```python
import pygeoinf0 as pg
# 1. the domain
D = pg.Box("[0, 1]", "[0, 1]")
# 2. the space on D: a form with its boundary conditions, then the space of that order
form = pg.SobolevForm(D, 1, length_scale=0.2, dirichlet=("x-",))
H = pg.Sobolev(form.operator(), 2)
```

Length scales and orders in the recipes are illustrative. A realisation (`realisation=` on a constructor) is the numerics,
not another space, so it is not a column; `how` names the one a space got.

The matrix is computed by trying every combination (`pg.catalogue.probe()`, seconds), stored as a snapshot in the
package and checked against a fresh probe by `tests/test_spaces_catalogue.py`; "ok" means constructed and a finite norm
of a bounded functional's representer, which shows that a space is usable, not that it is correct.


## Continuous functions: C([a, b])

The space whose elements most readers already know: functions with values, the sup norm, plots that are the function.

```python
import numpy as np, pygeoinf0 as pg
X = pg.Continuous("[0, 1]")                         # C([0, 1]); open or unbounded intervals are refused
f = X(lambda x: np.sin(6 * x) * np.exp(-x), resolution=0.1)   # its narrowest feature: f.claims state it met
f(0.3), f.norm(), f.error                           # a value; max |f| (exact for the realisation); est. sup error
try:
    X(lambda x: (x > 0.4).astype(float), resolution=0.1)
except pg.NotContinuous as e:                       # refused: it jumps by 1 at x = 0.4
    print(e)
X.evaluation(0.3)(f), X.average("[0.2, 0.6]")(f)    # bounded functionals: measures (norm = total mass)
G = pg.Integrals(X, [0.25, 0.5, "[0, 1]"])          # f -> (f(0.25), f(0.5), int_0^1 f): an operator into R^3
pg.Inclusion(X, pg.L2("[0, 1]"))(f)                 # C -> L2 (norm <= 1); L2 -> C does not exist (refused)
pg.Inclusion(pg.Sobolev("[0, 1]", 1, length_scale=0.2), X)   # H^s -> C for s > 1/2: the Sobolev embedding
```

What changes compared with L2: elements are functions, not classes (`f.redefine_at` is refused: other values make
another function); point evaluation is bounded; the dual is the space of finite measures (Riesz-Markov), so functionals
are given by measures, not Riesz representers. C has no inner product (the parallelogram law fails: `X.inner` refuses,
saying so), so `G.adjoint`, `G @ G.adjoint` and least squares do not exist on C: that is what the Hilbert spaces are
for. Continuity of a given function is checked on its realisation, not proved: a gap at a breakpoint above 1e-6 of the
function's size and ten local errors is a jump; jumps smaller than that, and cusps sharper than about |x - x0|^(1/4)
(which look like jumps at double precision), are the limits. Norms are the maximum of the realised polynomial (a grid in
arccos t refined by bisection on p', complete by Bernstein's inequality), errors are estimated in the sup norm. Not yet:
other domains (disks, balls: coordinate singularities at the centre need care), C^k, Gaussian measures on C (Wiener).

## Sobolev spaces, paths and ray tomography (overnight 2026-10-25/26)

(DESIGN.md, COMPETITORS.md). Notebook: `examples/tomography.ipynb`.

```python
import numpy as np, pygeoinf0 as pg
D = pg.Disk(1.0)
H = pg.Sobolev(D, 1.5, length_scale=0.1)            # H^{3/2}_l(D); print(H.claims)
th = np.linspace(0, 2 * np.pi, 24, endpoint=False)
rays = [pg.Segment((np.cos(a), np.sin(a)), (np.cos(b), np.sin(b))) for a in th for b in th[th > a + 0.5]]
n, sigma = len(rays), 0.01                           # rays are sets; their measure is arc length
Dd = pg.Euclidean(n, covariance=sigma ** 2)          # the whitened norm (route B): unit noise in Dd's metric; or a
                                                     # plain D with the noise stated, pg.Additive(G, N(0, sigma^2 I))
G = pg.Functionals(H.riesz(rays), into=Dd)
d = G(H(lambda x, y: np.exp(-((x - 0.3) ** 2 + y ** 2) / 0.05))) + Dd.standard_gaussian().sample(seed=1)
A = G @ G.adjoint
alpha = pg.meta.discrepancy(pg.Problem(mdr=G, datum=d)).gamma   # ||G m - d||^2 = n: a choice from the datum
m = G.adjoint((A + alpha * pg.Identity(Dd)).inv(d))  # damped least squares
m(0.2, 0.3); m.norm(); m.plot()
mL = pg.Inclusion(H, pg.L2(D))(m)                    # the same function in L2 (fit tolerance 1e-4; mL.error: reached)
post = pg.probability.cylindrical(H, alpha).condition(G, d)   # posterior mean = m (the same element: norms, distances)
post.std(np.array([[0.2, 0.3], [0.0, 0.0]]))         # exact posterior standard deviations of point values
post.std(pg.Segment((0, -0.5), (0, 0.5)))            # ... of a ray integral
H.riesz(pg.clip(pg.Segment((-2, 0.3), (2, 0.3)), D)) # rays that leave the domain are clipped first
C = pg.Circle(0.5)                                   # an interface: a set, so a functional (s > 1/2)
m.integral(C) / C.volume, post.std(C) / C.volume     # the mean over the interface, and its posterior std
```

**Arguments of `M.riesz` and `mu.std`.** A set (its natural measure: arc length, area, length) or a Dirac measure is
one functional; a list of them is a Family. A polyline with one density value per piece is
`pg.LebesgueMeasure(pg.Polyline(P)).with_density(w)` (`PieceDensity`: e.g. slowness-squared weights of a travel time);
other weighted measures (multiples of Dirac measures) are not parsed: scale the representer instead. Points are decided by shape against the domain's Cartesian dimension d: an array of shape (d,) is one point,
(m, d) is m points (on an interval a number is one point and a sequence of numbers m points).

**The prior's scale.** `pg.probability.cylindrical(H, a)` is N(0, I/a) with I the identity of H^s_l: the variance of a point value
is k(0)/a, with k(0) = Gamma(s - d/2) / (Gamma(s) (4 pi)^(d/2) l^d) for s > d/2 on domains of R^d and paths (15.9 for
s = 3/2, d = 2, l = 0.1; on circles, tori and spheres the kernel is a lattice or spectral sum and k(0) differs), and
the variance of a ray integral is the ray's energy over a. So alpha in the damped solution is exactly the inverse prior
scale when the data space carries the noise covariance, and `discrepancy` chooses it by chi^2 = n. (A point relation
into a covariance-weighted D reads unit noise in D's metric and says so in a claim; into a plain D it states no noise, and
`discrepancy` refuses with `pg.NoiseUnstated` unless `target=` is stated: plan 071 P4.)

**What the standard deviations mean.** They are exact for the stated prior, and only as good as that prior. With alpha
from the discrepancy principle and a length scale chosen by hand, a smooth true model of unit size is usually not a
plausible draw from N(0, I/alpha), and its error can be many posterior standard deviations (a naive-user test found 2
to 18 sigma, and about 1700 sigma at s = 1.0001 in a ball, where the ray energies are near the trace threshold and
alpha explodes). Choose s, l and the prior scale for the model class (`cylindrical(H, alpha, mean=m_ref)` takes a
reference model), and keep s away from the threshold (d - k)/2 of the data you use.

**Definitions** (one per kind of set; `spaces/sobolev.py`):
- domains of R^d (interval, box, disk, cylinder, ball) and open paths (in arc length): the restriction space
  H^s_l(X) = {U|_X : U in H^s_l(R^d)} with the quotient norm, where ||U||^2 = (2 pi)^-d int (1 + l^2|xi|^2)^s |U^|^2
  (McLean 2000, ch. 3). No boundary conditions, no padding. Its kernel is the Matern kernel; the Riesz representer of
  u -> int u dmu is k_mu = int K(., y) dmu(y), and <k_mu, k_nu> = int int K dmu dnu exactly;
- flat tori and circles: the spectral space sum (1 + l^2 lambda)^s |u_k|^2; by Poisson summation its kernel is the
  lattice sum of the Matern kernel, so the same exact machinery applies with images;
- spheres: the spectral (Laplace-Beltrami) space; representers of points and arcs are truncated at an adaptive degree
  with an estimated error (a path measure is not band-limited: algebraic convergence).
- s = 0 is L2 (`Sobolev(X, 0) == L2(X)`).

**Functionals are measures.** A point, a ray (`Segment`, `Polyline`), an arc on a sphere (`Arc`), a subinterval: their
natural measures define u -> int u dmu. `M.riesz(mu)` gives the Riesz representer, or refuses with the trace theorem:
integration over a k-dimensional set is bounded iff s > (d - k)/2 (rays: s > 1/2 in 2-D, s > 1 in 3-D; points:
s > d/2). L2 refuses all lower-dimensional measures. Supports must lie in the closure of the domain (rays that leave it
are refused: `pg.clip(ray, domain)` gives the exact chord); the check is exact on convex domains (ends and corners of
straight pieces suffice; spheres and circles by their extent), sampled elsewhere. There is no Schwartz space: it is
not a Hilbert (not even a normed) space, and every
distribution needed here is an element of the Hilbert scale H^-s through its representer.

**Geometry.** Paths and flat patches are Domains with fewer natural coordinates than Cartesian ones, like circles and
spheres: `Segment(p, q)`, `Patch(o, e1, e2)` (one class, `Flat`), `Polyline(points)` (vertices are reference breaks),
`Arc(sphere, p, q)`. They carry L2 and Sobolev spaces, measures and quadratures like any domain (functions on a ray,
interfaces such as spheres inside a ball).

**Numerics** (`realisations/rkhs/kernels.py`, `realisations/rkhs/backend.py`, `realisations/series/sphere_sobolev.py`): energies of points and straight pieces are
reduced exactly to one-dimensional analytic integrals (closed-form radial tail F(rho) = c l^2 (rho/l)^(nu+1)
K_(nu+1)(rho/l) for every Matern order; signed-triangle decomposition of the parallelogram of two segments; sinh
substitutions at near-singularities; the kernel singularity on the line by its expansion at 0). Checked against mpmath
(1e-14 to 1e-16 for the test configurations, half-integer and general Matern orders), against closed forms in 1-D and
the spectral definition on tori and circles, and by additivity and continuity laws; pairs of rays within 1e-8 radians
of parallel are accurate to about 1e-8. Gram matrices (europa, after the structure wave): 1000 rays in a square 1.5 s,
800 rays in a cube 1.0 s.

**What is not exact.** Norms of formulas (functions given by expressions) in a restriction space are minimal-extension
norms: not computed in 2-D and 3-D (`NormUnavailable`; exact on tori and circles, where the definition is spectral; on
intervals with integer s from derivatives, see "Which Sobolev space?"); inner products of
formulas with representers are int f dmu by Gauss quadrature along the supports (rounding level for smooth formulas;
f is assumed to be in H^s). `pg.probability.cylindrical(H, alpha)` is the cylindrical Gaussian N(0, I/alpha) on H: exact variances and
posteriors, no samples in H (its push-forwards to R^n can be sampled). L2 realisations of
representers (`Inclusion`) converge algebraically (kinks along the rays) and carry error estimates. On the sphere,
representers of paths are truncated (estimated error; exact zonal kernels are future work).

**Package structure:** one exceptions module (`core/errors.py`), an abstract `Backend` with required,
derived and optional (`Unsupported`) operations, one registry choosing every realisation (`realisations/registry.py`,
`realisation=` argument instead of environment switches; it also returns each domain's `Geometry`: flat coordinates,
periods and which definition of H^s applies, and holds the capability protocol: what a realisation can do is asked by
name, `registry.provides(R, "box_support")`, `capability(R, name)`, a missing one refused with
`errors.CapabilityMissing` naming it and what exists; plan 069 P3), measures as atoms of named kinds (`atoms.py`) whose energies and values
are one table indexed by the two kinds (`pairs.py`; the mathematics in `kernels.py` and `surfaces.py`), and mechanical
structure tests (`tests/test_structure.py`: module sizes reported over 500 lines, a soft rule (split by responsibility, not by count), layering, exceptions, construction of backends, no
`isinstance` on domain types in realisations/, operators/, probability/ and spaces/sobolev.py outside the registry and
the measure parsers; spaces/base.py, plotting and `clip` still dispatch on domain types; no type test on a realisation
class outside realisations/, a list that may only shrink). Adding a kind of support
(say, a circle inside a disk) means a `Kind` value, one branch in `atoms_from` with its sample points and extent, its
support (whose quadrature integrates formulas), one table row per partner kind, and its mathematics in `surfaces.py`.

**Surfaces as functionals** (`realisations/rkhs/surfaces.py`). The area measure of a flat `Patch` in a 3-D domain defines
u -> int u dA, bounded iff s > 1/2. The potential of a patch at a point is exact (the polygon reduction used for pairs of
segments); energies with segments and other patches are graded Gauss quadratures of it (breaks at its cusps, where a
ray crosses the patch or an edge), and a patch's own energy comes from the autocorrelation of the parallelogram, so it
is a 2-D integral with one singular corner. Each is computed at two orders: the difference is the error estimate
(`Family.errors()`; a `RealisationWarning` above 1e-6 relative). Against adaptive SciPy quadrature: 1e-16 to 1e-13 where the
reference is reliable (a point in the plane of the patch with a singular kernel: 3e-10, at the reference's own error
level); additivity over a split patch 1e-14. Cost per pair at one order: a patch's own energy 15-25 ms, a ray with a
patch 1-170 ms, two touching patches about 10 s, two crossing patches 30-50 s (three nested quadratures; separated
patches are cheaper); the error estimate needs the second order, about 1.75 times that. Spheres inside a ball (interfaces such as a core boundary) are
functionals too: the potential of a sphere is closed-form, g(r) = (2 pi R / r) int_|r-R|^(r+R) k(rho) rho drho, its own
energy 8 pi^2 R^2 G(0, 2R), and pairs with rays, patches and other spheres are one- or two-dimensional graded
quadratures of it (1e-16 to 1e-11 against SciPy; about 1 ms per ray, 2 s per patch). In 2-D, circles inside a disk
or box are interfaces too (arc length, bounded iff s > 1/2): their potential is a graded quadrature with an exact line
integral at the nearest point (1e-16 to 4e-12 against SciPy; inscribed N-gons converge to it at O(1/N^2) for s >= 3/2,
about N^-1.5 for s = 0.8); they were added as a check of the structure: a Kind value, one branch in `atoms_from` with
its samples and extent, one support, three rows of the table, three functions in `surfaces.py`. Other curved
surfaces are not implemented.


## Which Sobolev space? (plan 006, 2026-10-26)

The norm is part of the inverse problem: the same functions with another inner product give other representers,
adjoints, minimum-norm solutions and posteriors. A space states its construction (`H.construction`, `print(H.claims)`);
two constructions with the same inner product are the same space (`==`), different inner products different spaces.
Research record: `experiments/EXP-026-sobolev-constructions/NOTES.md` (derivations, independent checks, figures).

```python
import numpy as np, pygeoinf0 as pg
I, l = pg.Interval("[0, 1]"), 0.1
a = pg.SobolevForm(I, 1, length_scale=l)               # int u v + l^2 u' v' on H^1, natural Neumann
spaces = {"restriction": pg.Sobolev(I, 1, length_scale=l),          # H^1_l(R) restricted: stationary kernel
          "intrinsic": a.space(),                                   # (V, a) == pg.Sobolev(a.operator(), 1)
          "Dirichlet": pg.SobolevForm(I, 1, length_scale=l, dirichlet="all").space(),          # on H^1_0
          "Robin": pg.SobolevForm(I, 1, length_scale=l, robin={"right": 0.05}).space(),
          "spectral Neumann s=2": pg.Sobolev(a.operator(), 2),      # D(A_N), ||(1 - l^2 d^2) u||: u' = 0 at the ends
          "intrinsic order 2": pg.SobolevForm(I, 2, length_scale=l).space(),     # int u v + 2l^2 u'v' + l^4 u''v''
          "spectral Dirichlet s=1.5": pg.Sobolev(pg.SobolevForm(I, 1, length_scale=l, dirichlet="all").operator(), 1.5)}
for name, H in spaces.items():
    G = pg.Functionals(H.riesz([pg.DiracMeasure(0.05), pg.Interval(0.4, 0.6)]))
    m = G.adjoint((G @ G.adjoint).inv(G.codomain([1.0, 0.1])))      # the minimum-norm model fitting both data
    print(f"{name:26s} m(0) = {m(0.0):+.3f}   prior std at 0, 0.5: {pg.probability.cylindrical(H).std(np.array([0.0, 0.5]))}")
box = pg.Box("[0, 1]", "[0, 1]", "[0, 0.5]")              # boxes: spectral spaces with Neumann/Dirichlet faces, any s
Hb = pg.Sobolev(pg.SobolevForm(box, 1, length_scale=l, dirichlet=["z-"]).operator(), 2)
```

| construction | built by | elements | kernel near the boundary | prior std at an end (s = 1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| restriction | `pg.Sobolev(D, s, length_scale=l)` | all of H^s | stationary Matern; boundary values penalised (1-D, s = 1: `+ l [u(a)^2 + u(b)^2]`; s = 2: `+ 2l [u^2 + l u d_n u + l^2 (d_n u)^2]` at each end) | sqrt(k(0)) |
| intrinsic form | `pg.SobolevForm(D, k, length_scale=l).space()` | all of H^k | natural Neumann (d_n K = 0): reflected | sqrt(2 coth(1/l) k(0)), about sqrt(2) times |
| Dirichlet parts | `dirichlet="left"`, `["x-", "y+"]`, `"all"` | u = 0 there | vanishes; data there are refused (zero functional) | 0 |
| Robin parts | `robin={"right": beta}` | all of H^1 | `l^2 d_n u + beta u = 0`; beta = l is the restriction in 1-D | between |
| spectral power | `pg.Sobolev(a.operator(), s)`: D(A^(s/2k)) | conditions of order < s - 1/2 (Neumann: d_n u = 0 from s = 3/2; Dirichlet: u = 0 from s = 1/2) | Neumann images: 2, 4, 8 times the variance at faces, edges, corners of a box | as the form at s = k |

Which to choose. The restriction prefers models that continue smoothly outside D: right when the boundary is a
computational cut (a window on a larger Earth). The intrinsic form prefers the smallest interior amplitude and
gradient and lets the boundary values float (its prior variance doubles at a Neumann boundary): right when the model
exists only in D and nothing is known at the boundary. Dirichlet, Robin and mixed parts state boundary knowledge
(a fixed value, a penalised one). Spectral powers give fractional orders with boundary conditions, at the price of
conditions on every element above s = 3/2 (Neumann) or s = 1/2 (Dirichlet); at s = 2 spectral Neumann is the
subspace {d_n u = 0} of the intrinsic order-2 space with the same inner product, not the intrinsic space itself.

Realisations (exact): intervals, every construction (Matern kernel plus a finite-rank boundary correction for integer
orders, signed reflection images for fractional ones; norms of formulas from derivatives for integer orders); boxes
and rectangles, spectral spaces with Neumann/Dirichlet faces, any s (images; s = 1 is the intrinsic H^1 form). Disks,
balls, curved boundaries, Robin faces on boxes, fractional Robin powers and boundary data for order-2 forms are not
realised (`Unsupported`); the restriction is available everywhere. The operator of a form, `A = a.operator()`, knows
its natural conditions (`A.conditions(part)`); its domain with the graph-equivalent norm ||A u|| is
`A.domain_space()`.


## Layered planets and direct sums (plan 007, 2026-10-26)

A planet is a ball cut by concentric spheres (`pg.LayeredBall`: a `Ball` and `Shell`s, interfaces are `Sphere`s). A
model that may jump at the interfaces lives in a direct sum of spaces on the layers (the broken `pg.PiecewiseSpace` on
the ball's radial partition, a `pg.DirectSum`),
with interface topography in spaces on the spheres and finite-dimensional terms in `pg.Euclidean` summands. A datum is
one dict of (signed) measures per summand; rays are split at the interfaces by the layered space itself, and the
user code is the ladder's. Research record and checks: `experiments/EXP-027-layered-moon/NOTES.md`; the synthetic
Moon (SensRay, TauP): `examples/moon_tomography.py`.

```python
import numpy as np, pygeoinf0 as pg
P = pg.LayeredBall([0.2, 0.6, 0.97, 1.0], names=["core", "lower", "upper", "crust"], interfaces=["cmb", "mid", "moho"])
V = pg.PiecewiseSpace.sobolev(P, 2, length_scale=0.1, pieces=["lower", "upper", "crust"])   # independent across interfaces
X = pg.DirectSum({"v": V, "moho": pg.Sobolev(P.interface("moho"), 2, length_scale=0.2)}, weights={"moho": 0.5})
rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
data = []
while len(data) < 30:                                   # chords between surface points that miss the core
    a, b = (u / np.linalg.norm(u) for u in rng.normal(size=(2, 3)))
    if a @ b < -0.85:
        continue
    datum = {"v": -1.0 * pg.LebesgueMeasure(pg.Polyline([a, b])).with_density([1.0])}       # -int dv ds
    moho = [c.point for c in P.crossings([a, b]) if c.interface == "moho"]
    if moho:
        datum["moho"] = sum(0.1 * pg.DiracMeasure(x) for x in moho)                           # + sum c h(x)
    data.append(datum)
D = pg.Euclidean(len(data), covariance=0.01 ** 2)
truth = X({"v": {k: (lambda x, y, z: 0.1 * x) for k in V.names}, "moho": lambda x, y, z: z})
G = pg.Functionals(X.riesz(data), into=D)
d = D(G(truth).values + 0.01 * rng.normal(size=len(data)))
alpha = pg.meta.discrepancy(pg.Problem(mdr=G, datum=d)).gamma
post = pg.probability.cylindrical(X, alpha).condition(G, d)
m = post.mean                                           # m["v"]["crust"], m["moho"]: parts, elements of their spaces
values = V.evaluate(m["v"], np.array([[0, 0, 0.985], [0, 0, 0.97]]), side="above")   # on the moho: say the side
std = post.std({"v": np.array([[0, 0, 0.985], [0, 0, 0.5]])})
```

What is exact and why (NOTES.md): restriction energies of measures in a closed layer do not depend on the layer, so a
layer's Gram matrix is the ray Gram of the pieces in it, and G G* is the sum over summands of their Gram matrices over
their weights (`realisations/series/direct_sum.py` stores, per summand, only the data that touch it). A polyline never lies in
an interface (a chord between two points of a sphere passes inside it), so ray pieces need no side; points on an
interface do (`side="below"/"above"`). The interface term of a travel time is sum c h(x) over the crossings,
c = n . (p_in - p_out): +eta for each leg below, -eta for each leg above (checked against TauP and exact travel
times). `pg.GaussianMeasure.product` gives products of measures on the summands; `pg.BlockDiagonal` operators act
summand by summand.

The same objects carried to the pieces of a planet (restricted measures, partitions, (field, piece) sums, Sobolev
fields glued or broken) are the next section.

## Fields on the pieces of a domain (plan 035, 2026-11-03)

A function lives on Omega; Omega is cut into pieces Omega_k (a ball into shells, an interval at points, a sphere into
latitude bands) and the one space is replaced by a sum of spaces on the pieces, one summand per field and piece. The
elements, measures and functionals are carried to the pieces in one way, and what holds depends on the space: the
table is the package's statement (each entry is a claim on the object, a theorem, asserted with a reference, or
checked; what fails is refused by name).

A realised element restricted to a piece is cut, not refitted, where its realisation allows it: exact, with the source's error
estimate (`Restrict`'s claim "realisation" says which). That covers piecewise Legendre on intervals, radial x harmonic on concentric
shells, tensor bases on smaller boxes of the same chart and cell values on whole cells. Others are evaluated on the piece and fitted. Gluing and extension by zero join realised
parts the same way, with no fit: the result is the piecewise function, jumps at seams included.

```python
import math, numpy as np, pygeoinf0 as pg
S = pg.Sphere(1.0)
P = pg.Partition(S, {"north": S.cap(0.6), "belt": S.band(0.6, 2.0), "south": S.cap(2.0, pole="south")})
H = pg.Sobolev(S, 2, length_scale=0.3)
M = pg.PiecewiseSpace(P, {"vp": pg.L2, "rho": H})         # vp on the three pieces, rho glued (H itself)
x = M.split({"vp": lambda x, y, z: 1 + z, "rho": lambda x, y, z: z})
band = pg.LebesgueMeasure(S.band(0.3, 1.5))                # across the seam theta = 0.6
print(M.names, M.functional("vp", band)(x))               # = int_band (1 + z) dA, split over north and belt
seam = pg.DiracMeasure(np.array(S.from_angles(0.6, 1.0)).ravel())
print(M.functional("rho", seam)(x))                        # glued: no side= needed (a claim)
try:
    M.parts("vp", seam)                                    # a point on a seam of a sum over pieces: say the side
except ValueError as e:
    print(e)
C = pg.Continuous("[0, 1]")
Q = pg.Partition("[0, 1]", ["[0, 0.4]", "[0.4, 1]"], names=["a", "b"])
u = C.element(lambda t: np.sin(3 * t))
print(max(C.restrict_to(Q[n]).codomain.norm(C.restrict_to(Q[n])(u)) for n in Q), C.norm(u))   # ||u|| = max_k
print(Q.functionals(C, pg.DiracMeasure(0.4), side="below")(u), u(0.4))                       # a seam mass once
```

**Measures** (`measures/restrict.py`). `mu.restricted(D, side=None)` is f dmu -> f|_D dmu|_D, or None when mu does not
charge D. Volume, density and bank measures restrict to the exact set S cap D (`domains/pieces.py`: a ball cut by a
ball or shell about another centre is a `BallPiece`, which keeps both descriptions, so containment is decided, not
sampled; a bank weight first narrows S to its support; bands of a sphere meet in bands); paths are cut at the spheres
of balls and shells and great-circle arcs at the parallels of bands; points and sphere measures on the boundary of D
need `side=` (never halved); signed measures restrict termwise. On a direct sum the L2 parts on S cap D stay symbolic
(`realisations/series/direct_sum.Symbolic`) and pair exactly as in their summand, G G* = sum_k gram(l_k) / w_k.

**Partitions** (`domains/partition.py`). `pg.Partition(Omega, pieces)` checks the tiling where it is decided exactly:
radial intervals of concentric balls and shells, coordinate intervals of an interval, boxes in a box, and colatitude
intervals of a sphere (`S.band(theta0, theta1)`, `S.cap(theta)`, `S.cap(theta, pole="south")`: bands and polar caps
about the sphere's z axis, `domains/bands.py`); caps about other axes, polygons, disks and non-concentric balls are
refused (Unsupported), gaps and overlaps are `pg.NotATiling`. It owns the seams with their two sides (below: nearer
the centre, the lower coordinate, the smaller colatitude), refines as a tree (`P.refine`, `P.flat()`), splits measures
(`P.split(mu, side=)`, with a claim that the parts sum to mu) and gives the operators `X.restrictions(P)` and `P.glue(X)`.
`LayeredBall.partition` is its radial one; `Covariance.glued` decides its tiling with it.

**Elements**: `pg.Restrict(X, D)` (= `X.restrict_to(D)`) is u -> u|_D into a new space X(D) (X is never changed):
formulas are evaluated on D, symbolic representers restrict their measures, realised elements are evaluated and
refitted (claim "realisation"). `pg.PiecewiseSpace(P, {"vp": pg.L2, "rho": H, ...}, broken=)` (`spaces/piecewise.py`,
plan 069 P7: it replaced `Layered` and `OnPieces`) is the flat (field, piece) sum keyed `"vp_<piece>"` (a glued Sobolev
field: keyed by the field), with `M.functional(field, mu)` (a list of measures: `pg.Functionals` into R^n), `M.parts`,
`M.piece_functionals`, `M.split({...})`, `M.glue(x)`, `M.glued()`, `M.seam_jumps(x)` and `M.evaluate(x, points, side=)`;
each field follows its own row below, so L2 fields, glued and broken Sobolev fields mix in one sum
(`test_pieces_p5.py`: their Gram is block diagonal with each field's own energy).
One field: `pg.PiecewiseSpace(P, X)` keyed by piece, `X.restrictions(P).codomain` is
`pg.PiecewiseSpace(P, X, broken=True)`, and `PiecewiseSpace.from_pieces(P, {piece: space})`, `.lebesgue(P)` and
`.sobolev(P, s, l)` build one space per piece (P a Partition or a LayeredBall). The mathematics: broken is the direct
sum of the fields on the pieces, with no relation across the seams, and its norm is the sum of the pieces' norms; glued
is the subspace whose pieces are the restrictions of one function, for H^s the interface conditions on the seams (none
for s < 1/2, continuity of the trace for 1/2 < s < 3/2 by the trace theorem, the normal derivatives too beyond); for L2
glued and broken are the same space (no trace, null seams). Gluing a broken H^s element checks the traces at points
of each seam by the precision rule (1/2 < s < 3/2) and refuses where they differ or where the condition is not
checked (`spaces/gluing.py`).

Sobolev in detail (`operators/pieces_sobolev.py`). For the restriction construction `H.restrict_to(D)` maps into
H^s(D), the same construction with the same kernel (energies of measures in D agree in both spaces); a representer of
a measure in the closure of D keeps its measures, anything else is evaluated and fitted on D (on intervals of integer
order its norm is the exact quotient norm, e.g. ||K(., y)|_[a, b]||^2 = e^(-2 (y - b)/l) K(b, b) for s = 1).
`pg.PiecewiseSpace(P, {"vp": H})` is glued by default (the author's decision): the summand is H itself, a functional is the
whole measure = the sum of its restrictions, and `broken=True` (or a set of fields) gives (+)_k H^s(Omega_k), keyed
"vp_<piece>", independent across the seams, with `side=` required there. A segment across both cuts of a ball (H^2,
l = 0.3): glued 0.3518 (its whole-ball energy), broken 0.1747. On a sphere (spectral, spherical harmonics) only the
glued view exists: an arc across two parallels has its whole-sphere energy, and the 3 x 3 Gram of its pieces sums to
it with cross terms. An m-dimensional measure is bounded on a piece by the same threshold as on Omega, s > (d - m)/2.

On the sphere, L2 pieces are realised in the theta x phi tensor basis (spherical harmonics are the whole sphere's).
`P.glue(L2(S))` into the tensor realisation is a piecewise formula with the seams as breaks (exact); spherical
harmonics take no breaks, so the glue keeps the measures v dA on the bands symbolic (exact pairings and values,
refused where a fit would cross the seams).

Explanation, no API (plan 035 D6, not decided): a Gaussian prior with covariance (1 - l^2 Delta)^-s on L2(Omega) has
the Sobolev space of that operator as its Cameron-Martin space. One covariance on the whole ball seen on the shells
(`Covariance.glued`) is the glued model, correlated across interfaces; an independent covariance per shell (seam
conditions on each) is the broken model; the jump or no-jump choice at the interfaces is that choice of model.


## Relations, states and cells (plan 008, 2026-10-26)

The elements framework of the thesis in the package. The model-data and model-property relations are relations between
spaces of point, set or measure kind (what R(x) is: an element, a subset, a probability measure); a state on a space
(of kind set, measure or ordering: `core/kinds.py`; the prior none is the whole space, a set, and a point is the
singleton set or the Dirac measure) is pushed forward through a relation, or updated by a datum through one, by a
cell of the state-by-relation table. Design record with the mathematics and its proofs:
`experiments/EXP-028-relations/DESIGN.md`; what the implementation found: `NOTES.md` there.

```python
import numpy as np, pygeoinf0 as pg
M = pg.Sobolev("[0, 1]", 1, length_scale=0.1)
D = pg.Euclidean(12, covariance=0.02 ** 2)                             # D's standard Gaussian is the noise
G = pg.Functionals(M.riesz([pg.Interval(a, a + 0.08) for a in np.linspace(0, 0.92, 12)]) * 12.5, into=D)
T = pg.Functionals(M.riesz([pg.Interval(0.45, 0.55)]) * 10.0)          # the MPR: the average over [0.45, 0.55]
d = G(M(lambda x: np.sin(2 * np.pi * x))) + D.standard_gaussian().sample(seed=1)

exact, bounded = pg.Exact(G), pg.Additive(G, pg.NormBall(D, 5.0))      # d = G m;  d in G m + B (B fixed before d)
gaussian = pg.Additive(G, D.standard_gaussian())                       # d ~ N(G m, 0.02^2 I)
K0 = pg.update(pg.NoPrior(M), exact, d)        # the fibre m-dagger + ker G; K0.summary(): the minimum-norm model
K1 = pg.update(pg.NormBall(M, 3.0), bounded, d)                        # the feasible set of BGP
lo, hi = pg.push(K1, T).bounds(0)              # certified bounds: primal witness <= sup <= dual certificate
prior = pg.probability.cylindrical(M, 1.0)                             # N(0, I) on M, named by the caller
post = pg.update(prior, gaussian, d)                                   # the same as prior.condition(G, d)
pli = pg.push(post, T)                                                 # the property posterior (PLI)
E = pg.Span([D(np.r_[np.ones(6), np.zeros(6)]), D(np.r_[np.zeros(6), np.ones(6)])])
R = pg.Additive(G, E)                          # an unknown offset per group of data: a linear relation, mul R = E
m_free = R.converse().operator_part()(d)       # the minimum-norm model with the offsets free (the quotient D / E)
table = pg.cells.table()                       # every cell: kinds, output, hypotheses, status
```

**Relations** (`core/relation.py`, `relations/`). `pg.LinearOperator` is a relation of point kind (no change in
behaviour; `G(S)` of a set and `G(mu)` of a measure push them forward, `G @ R` composes with any relation).
`pg.Map(X, Y, f, dom=...)` is a possibly partial, nonlinear map; `pg.SetValued(X, Y, values=... | member=...)` a
set-valued relation given by its values or its graph; `pg.MarkovKernel(X, Y, law)` an observation kernel. A relation needs no
forward operator; `pg.Additive(G, noise)` and `pg.Exact(G)` are conveniences that build one and say so in its claims:
a subset as noise gives `G m + S` (convex and closed graphs derived from S and G), a subspace gives a linear relation, a
Gaussian measure a kernel, `nuisance=E` with Gaussian noise the kernel on the quotient D / E (free nuisances such as
origin times: equal to the flat-prior limit, DESIGN 1.7). `R.converse()` reverses the graph (a kernel refuses without a
prior: `L.converse(prior=mu)` is the posterior kernel; `L.acceptance(alpha)` gives confidence-set inversion;
`L.likelihood(d)` an ordering by likelihood). `pg.stack(R1, R2, rule=...)` needs a stated rule ("all", "any",
("at least", k), "independent" for kernels); its operator is the column x -> (G_k x)_k
(`pg.operators.ColumnOperator`) into the direct sum of the data spaces, a Euclidean with block labels.

**The data space and the noise** (plan 068 P5, decisions Q1 as amended on 2026-11-15 and Q9; plan 071 P4). Either
route works, and they are one model:

- A, a plain data space with the noise stated separately: `D = pg.Euclidean(n)` or `pg.DataSpace(n, labels=...)` (R^n
  with the dot product and labelled coordinates; a `DataSpace` refuses a metric), and the noise in the relation,
  `pg.Additive(G, pg.GaussianMeasure(D, C, mean=b))`.
- B, the whitened norm: `D = pg.Euclidean(n, covariance=C)`, whose metric is C^-1, with `D.standard_gaussian()` as the
  noise (its coordinates are N(0, C)). Not `pg.GaussianMeasure(D, pg.Diagonal(sigma ** 2, D))` on this D: a covariance
  operator is read in D's inner product, so that one has coordinate covariance sigma^4.

The posterior, PLI, SOLA, damped least squares and the discrepancy principle give the same results by both routes to
rounding (`tests/test_laws.py`, `law_both_data_routes_give_the_same_inference`); route A is read in the noise's inner
product where a method needs that (`R.standardised()`: D's vectors with the C^-1 inner product, where the noise is the
standard Gaussian). A point relation `G` (exact data) states no noise: where a method or choice rule needs a noise law
(sola, `pg.meta.discrepancy` without `target=`, `maximum_evidence`, `knee`, `pb.decomposition()`), a covariance-weighted
D means unit noise in its metric (claimed), and a plain D is refused with `pg.NoiseUnstated` naming both routes. A
covariance that is not symmetric beyond rounding (`pygeoinf0.spaces.metrics.symmetric_within_rounding`), a variance
that is not positive or a matrix that is not positive definite is refused with `pg.NotACovariance`. `D.whitened(C)`
names the whitened coordinates of a noise covariance C: the isometry d -> L^-1 d (C = L L^T) from D's vectors with the
C^-1 inner product onto plain R^n, with `W.inv = W.adjoint`. A direct sum of Euclideans is a Euclidean:
`pg.spaces.euclidean_sum({"P": D1, "S": D2}, weights=...)` has the sum's inner product and block labels
(`E.select(block="S")`); `pg.DirectSum` keeps tuple elements for sums with function spaces.

**Linear relations** (`pg.LinearRelation(G, mul=E, dom=V)`): dom, ran, ker, mul, the adjoint (always closed:
mul R* = dom^perp), the converse (its operator part is the minimum-norm map: `G.converse().operator_part()(d)` is
`G.adjoint((G @ G.adjoint).inv(d))`), `quotient()`, and `domain_space()`, the graph-norm space. The ambient space
(`R.domain`), the restricted domain (`R.dom`, a subset) and the graph-norm space are three objects;
the closed, densely defined operators (`pygeoinf0.operators.unbounded`: `pg.SobolevForm(...).operator()`,
`SturmLiouville`, `pg.Laplacian`) are themselves such relations (`ClosedOperator`): `A.dom` is D(A), an `OperatorDomain`
that is dense and, A being unbounded, not closed (no projection onto it: NotDefined), and `A.domain_space()` the
graph-norm space where it is built (plan 068 P3).

**States** (plan 067 P4): a state has a space, a kind its class declares and claims (`pg.State`, `pg.state_kind`). The
kinds are closed (`core/kinds.py`): set, measure and ordering (declared, not developed). `pg.NoPrior(M)` (the prior
none) is `pg.Whole(M)`, a set; a point is the singleton set or the Dirac measure (`pg.states.as_set(x)`,
`pg.states.as_measure(x)`), and the cells accept an element as the asserted point (a selected model is a summary, not
a state). Conversions between kinds take their choice as an argument (`pg.states.as_set(mu, form=, mass=)`,
`pg.states.harden`). States are a subset, a measure (`GaussianMeasure`; a cylindrical one speaks about bounded
functionals only) or an `pg.Ordering` (a cost with its feasible set). A subset of a space is a `pygeoinf0.sets.Subset` (a Set: `x in S` is
`bool(S.member(x))` and raises on UNKNOWN; `&` and `|` with the one `pg.EmptySet(X)`); the convex sets with a support
function are `pygeoinf0.convex.ConvexSet`: `pg.NormBall` (Gram-exact in any Hilbert space), `pg.Ellipsoid`, `pg.Slab`,
`pg.CoordinateBox`, `pg.Polytope` (in R^n), `pg.AffineSubset`, `pg.Span`; sums, images, unions, intersections;
membership is three-valued (`pg.Truth`, UNKNOWN never reads as FALSE; a Truth compared with a bool by `==` or `!=`
raises `pg.errors.TruthComparedWithBool`: test `t is pg.Truth.TRUE`, plan 071 P1). Tolerance is not truth: a ball's
membership and `f.equals(g)` are TRUE only at a computed excess or distance 0, UNKNOWN at the rounding level.
`S.support(v)` is an `Enclosure` (plan 067 P3): its lower end is a lower bound and its upper end an upper bound of
sigma_S(v), and the set algebra keeps each end in its direction (a subspace's support is 0 only where v's component in
it computes to 0, [0, +inf] at the rounding level, +inf above it); `S.interval(k)` is the certified outer interval of a coordinate. The names do not shadow the domains
`pg.Ball` and `pg.Box`: `pg.Ball(space, r)` is refused with a pointer to `pg.NormBall(space, r)`.

**Cells** (`pg.push(state, R)`, `pg.update(prior, R, d)`; `pg.cells.table()`). Each cell checks the claims it needs
(strict by default: `pg.HypothesisUnmet` names them and how to state one, `pg.assert_claim(obj, key, reason)`;
`policy="tagged"` marks the result incomplete) and records them on the result. Undefined combinations refuse with the
reason (`pg.Refused`): a kernel without a prior, a set prior with a kernel (use acceptance regions), a set pushed through
a kernel (a family of measures), a measure through a set-valued relation (a random set). The existing methods are cells:
`condition` (B10, same numbers), `G(mu)` and `mu.std` (F10), the minimum-norm model (B5 with the whole space, B11), damped least squares
with a stated gamma (B13, B14; a gamma chosen from the datum by `pg.meta.discrepancy` carries its meta record). Feasible sets carry their dual: BGP bounds
are exact for norm-ball priors with ball, ellipsoid, slab or exact noise and free nuisances (certificates in E^perp:
event weights that sum to zero), a numerical bracket for boxes, polytopes and stacked blocks; an empty feasible set is a
finding with a Farkas certificate. With exact data or no prior, data directions at the rounding floor (dependent to
rounding, not by structure) are refused with the choices named; `K.dependent = True` declares the data exactly dependent.
With no prior a target is bounded only if it lies in the span of the data functionals; a target built from them
(`G.adjoint(y)`, `G[k].kernel`, `L @ G`) is so by construction, another one is declared with `K.bound(c, estimable=True)`
(or `K.image(T, estimable=True)`); otherwise a target there only to rounding gets an infinite bracket.

**Problems and methods** (`pg.Problem(M=..., D=..., mdr=..., P=..., mpr=..., prior=..., calibration=..., datum=...)`,
every element optional): `pb.methods()` says which methods run and what the others need, `pb.run(name)` runs one
(`pg.inference`: minimum_norm, least_squares, sola, csola, ocsola, bgp, pli), `pb.update()`, `pb.infer()`; calibration
statements (`pg.Deterministic`, `pg.Frequentist`, `pg.Bayesian`) are recorded and their hypotheses checked where the
library can; every result of `pb.run` carries them (`pb.calibration_claims()`), and a method refuses a calibration or an
option it does not declare (`pg.inference.METHODS[name].calibrations`, `.options`; plan 070 P5). Example: `examples/relations_three_noises.py` (one data set under exact, bounded and Gaussian noise;
figures in `results/figures/relations/`).

Not implemented (refused or `Unsupported` with the reason): the family-of-measures and random-set kinds, Gaussian
conditioning on a feasible set (a truncated Gaussian), orderings other than the norm cost, the inf-projection through a
set-valued relation, Chapman-Kolmogorov for non-Gaussian kernels (pointwise convex sets on function spaces: plan 009 P5, below).


## One data-space decomposition per problem (plan 009 P1, 2026-10-27)
`dec = pg.decompose(G, noise, middle)` whitens the data Gram matrix <g_i, S g_j> by the noise covariance and
decomposes it once (per block when the Gram operator or the values are block diagonal; `pg.BlockDiagonal(D, blocks,
shared=..., shift=..., order=...)` stores repeated blocks once). Every method is then a sum over its eigenvalues:
`dec.sola(T, gammas, d)` (estimates, noise std, delivered mismatch and their derivatives for a whole array of gamma),
`dec.posterior(T, d, a, nu)` (PLI for arrays of prior and noise scales), `dec.ellipsoid_bound(T, a, r, d, q)` (BGP
with certificates, O(n) per direction), `dec.feasibility(d, a=..., r=...)` (rho_min(a), a*), `dec.evidence(d, a, nu)`,
`dec.damping_for_misfit(d, target)`, `dec.predictive(d, a, nu)`. `pb.run("sola", gamma=array)`, `pli`, `bgp`, `ocsola` and `csola`
use the decomposition cached on the problem (`pb.decomposition()`); feasible sets with ball or ellipsoid noise use one
for all their bounds; `solvers.damping_for_misfit` (the root of the discrepancy principle) gives the same bits as before.
The engine is internal (`solvers/dataspace/`, plan 070 P3): it takes its metric explicitly (`D.whitened(C)`, the noise
covariance `C`, or `D`'s own inner product) and refuses to infer one; `pg.decompose` reads the noise law and states it. Data-space relations: `R.select(rows)`
(the marginal: exact deletion of data), `pg.Additive(G, noise, offset=b)` (a known correction),
`pg.Euclidean(n, labels={...})` with `select` and `groups`, `pg.meta.fit_noise` (noise laws by the evidence, a
meta rule; plan 070 P2). Background: experiments/EXP-030-cmb-thesis/DESIGN.md (G2, G3, G9).


## Rotation-aware spaces and property families (plan 009 P2, 2026-10-27)
`pg.L2(pg.Ball(R), realisation="radial_harmonics")` (also Shell and layered balls) realises f = sum_lm f_lm(r) Y_lm
with piecewise-Legendre radial factors (interfaces are breakpoints) and exact r^2 dr inner products. It is an explicit
option: "tensor" stays the default, so existing results are unchanged. `pg.BandProjection(space, degrees)` is the
orthogonal projection onto a band of degrees (idempotent, self-adjoint). Harmonic conventions are explicit:
`pygeoinf0.spaces.harmonic.THESIS` (a `pg.HarmonicConvention`; Condon-Shortley phase, (-1)^m) and a sqrt 2 scale variant convert coefficients exactly.
`pg.Tabulated(domain, nodes, values, rule=...)` is a function given by a table: "constant" on cells, "linear", or
the midpoint-linear rule with segment splits and flat/zero/linear extrapolation; its integrals are exact piecewise
and its breakpoints go to the realisation (`pg.realisations.Trapezoid(n)` is an explicit trapezoid option with a
stated error). `pg.Functionals(M, kernels, against=nu, taper=...)` forms f -> int k f dnu for any measure nu with a
`density_against(base)` method (Radon-Nikodym derivative and its singular behaviour, measures/densities.py);
`spaces/dual.l2_density` decides whether the representers are in L2 and refuses with `pg.Refused` otherwise (k ~
r^p near 0 on L2(r^2 dr) iff p > 1/2), by the same local rule as the boundedness decision of EXP-031 below. `pg.Functionals.separable(space, radial, harmonics)` builds functionals
k_i(r) Y_(l_i j_i); its `structured_gram(S)` and `structured_cross(T, S)` return `pg.BlockDiagonal` matrices with one
block per (l, j), shared where kernels repeat, and rotation invariance of S is derived from its factors (Schur).
`pg.CapAverages(M, radius, centres, profile=..., band=...)` and `pg.RotatedFamily(functional, centres)` are families
T = Phi tau (Funk-Hecke factors): `dec.sola`, `dec.posterior`, `dec.ellipsoid_intervals` and the methods sola, pli and
bgp work on the blocks and synthesise, never forming a p x p matrix (a lazy covariance, `.variances`); for plain maps
pli returns a low-rank lazy covariance above 2000 properties. BGP over a family solves all directions at once (Newton
on the secular equation, certified as for one direction). Background: EXP-030 DESIGN.md (G1, G2, G4, G10, G13).

Plan 009 P4a (2026-10-28) added two small general pieces and one fix found by the thesis's CMB problem (EXP-030
README.md). `pg.realisations.Trapezoid(n, over=(a, b))` is one rule on a parent interval restricted to sub-intervals,
so a computation that used one grid across the layers of a decomposed space is reproduced summand by summand;
`pg.Functionals.separable(..., quadrature={summand: rule})` chooses a rule per summand. Fix: the eigenvalues that BGP
and the feasibility closed forms treat as zero are now cut relative to their own block's largest eigenvalue (1e-15,
about 5 eps), and the feasibility secular functions use every eigenvalue; a cut relative to the global maximum
discarded real eigenvalues of blocks whose scales differ by many orders (on the CMB problem rho_min was off by 7e-8
and BGP endpoints by up to 5e-5 km; now both agree with the thesis's direct solves to rounding).


## Weighted measures (EXP-031, 2026-10-27/28)
`pg.LebesgueMeasure(S).with_density(w)` (the same object as `pg.DensityMeasure(pg.LebesgueMeasure(S), w)`) is w dsigma
on any set S: an interval, a domain used as a sub-domain (Box, Disk, Ball, ...), a path or surface, a `pg.Simplicial`
set, a `pg.Curve` or a `pg.Surface`; w a number, one value per piece, an element of a space on S, or a callable of the
Cartesian coordinates (measures/weights.py; `singular_at=points, exponent=a` declares |w| ~ dist^(-a)). Signed weights give
finite signed measures (said in the claims); `mu.mass()`, `mu.normalised()` (an average), `mu.density_against(lam)`
(plan 009's interface of `against=`: a Density with local orders, `.weight` the derivative as a weight). A
`pg.Tabulated` is an exact weight on intervals inside its domain. One decision says where u -> int u dmu is bounded
(spaces/dual.py: the trace excess `trace_excess(s, d, k)` = s - (d - k)/2, the one trace rule, and the weight's L^p class on H^s; d mu/d lambda in L^p' on L^p,
with exact local orders on intervals; L^1 on C), with one refusal path (`pg.Refused`); plan 009's density functionals
(`against=`, `l2_density`) use the same local rule. In L2 the
representers of measures of full dimension are kept symbolic, d mu/d lambda, with exact pairings
(spaces/representers.py); `M.average(S, weight=None)` on L2 and H^s; densities on subintervals of `pg.Continuous`.

Spaces with a density on 2-D and 3-D domains (plan 064). `pg.L2(pg.LebesgueMeasure(S).with_density(c))` for a constant
c > 0 on any domain with an L2 realisation (sphere, ball, shell, disk, cylinder, box, ...): the same classes of
functions as L2(S), with every pairing times c, in every realisation and at no extra cost; harmonic bases stay
orthogonal. So the CMB pairing varpi/(4 pi) int (h/r)(h'/r) dOmega is the space's own measure,
`pg.L2(pg.LebesgueMeasure(pg.Sphere(1)).with_density(1 / (4 * pi * r ** 2)))`, with a dimensionless direct-sum weight
varpi; a volume summand varpi/V int f g dx is the density 1/V. Every consumer of the measure takes the factor: inner
products, integrals, errors (sqrt(c)), L^p norms, representers of measures ((dnu/dx)/c), adjoints, pointwise-box
supports, sphere cells, oCSOLA's box rule, CapAverages and rotated families, separable functionals and Discretisation
pairings. Functionals given as measures (averages, cap averages) and functionals given by kernel values
(pg.Functionals.separable, the normal-mode forward maps) integrate against the domain's own measure lambda and do not
change with the density: only their representers do, k dlambda/dmu (plan 066). Functionals given by elements
(pg.Functionals(elements)) are inner products in the space's measure. `with_density(f, radial=True)` on a ball or shell is a radial
density rho(r) dx (f a callable of the distance from the centre), realised with `realisation="radial_harmonics"`:
rho is fitted once on the radial interval and the pairings sum_lj int f_lj g_lj rho r^2 dr stay exact for the
realisation; separable functionals still integrate against r^2 dr (their representers are k / (c rho)). Refused, with the reason: other densities on 2-D/3-D domains
(a full Gram), a radial density in the tensor or local realisation, Sobolev spaces with a density on these domains,
the Laplacian's Bessel-potential covariances on a weighted ball (their eigenfunctions are orthonormal for dx).

| pieces | same piece | touching / overlapping pairs |
|---|---|---|
| segments, curves | autocorrelation (graded, head from k's expansion); chart route | same segment, common vertex (Duffy) |
| spheres | geodesic polar coordinates | - |
| boxes, parallelograms, patches | Duffy on the autocorrelation (2-D, 3-D) | rectangles/boxes: cross-correlation with Duffy at 0; same parallelogram |
| triangles, tetrahedra | identical-panel split of T - T (hexagon; cone decomposition) | common edge, vertex (relative coordinates); common face, edge, vertex of tetrahedra (join coordinates) |

## Priors: Sturm-Liouville spectra, multiplication, covariance algebra, hardening (plan 009 P3, 2026-10-28)
`pg.Multiplication(M, a)` is f -> a f on an L2 space, a a Function, a `pg.Tabulated` (of x, or of r on a ball or shell

## Pointwise sets and box BGP (plan 009 P5, 2026-10-30)
`pg.PointwiseBox(X, lower=l, upper=u)` is {m : l <= m <= u a.e.} in L2 of a measure (on a direct sum: per summand, a
dict of bounds; missing summands are unbounded); `envelope=A, scale=a` is the weighted L-infinity ball {|m| <= a A},
`lower=0` the positivity cone. It is closed and convex (bounded when the bounds are finite), and its support function
is the L1-type integral sigma_B(q) = int (u q+ - l q-) dmu, attained by u on {q > 0} and l on {q < 0} (THEOREM).
`B.support(q, rtol=None)` returns the Enclosure of sigma_B(q) with certified lower and upper ends: exact on intervals
(the roots of each piece of q's piecewise-Legendre realisation; bounds constant or elements), a certified bracket on
the sphere and on balls and shells (constant bounds per summand: split a space at an envelope's steps, as for envelope
zones), exact in R^n; its value is the upper end. `B.projected(P)` is P(B) with sigma(q) = sigma_B(P* q).
`pg.Ellipsoid(X, r, shape=A, inverse=A^-1)` is {<A (m - c), m - c> <= r^2} in any Hilbert space (sigma = <q, c> +
r <q, A^-1 q>^(1/2)); in R^n `pg.Ellipsoid(D, r, covariance=S)` is unchanged.

The L1 integrals (realisations/bounds/sphere_l1.py). Zonal functions: exact from the real roots of the Legendre polynomial
(`zonal_l1`, and `zonal_sign_moments` for the extremal field u sign(p)). A band-limited g of degree n on the sphere:
p = g cos(latitude) is a trigonometric polynomial of degree (n + 1, n) on the torus, so cell integrals are exact
(Fourier), Bernstein bounds the derivatives by G >= sup |g|, a cell is sign-definite when |p(c)| exceeds its linear and
quadratic variation (then int |p| = |int p|), and an undecided cell is bracketed by the exact int |linearisation| +- the
quadratic remainder; cells are split until the bracket is within rtol (gap O(h^3) overall), one function and one chunk
of cells at a time (bounded memory). `l1_bracket(C, L, rtol)`: 1e-6 relative in about 1.5 s for degree 14.
Radial x harmonic fields: per radial piece by convexity in r (`radial_l1_bracket` for piecewise-Legendre factors;
`affine_radial_l1_bracket` when the factors are affine on known pieces times a positive weight, from the weight's
moments: Jensen below, the chord above). `harmonic_cell_integrals(L, lat_edges, lon_edges)` gives int_cell Y_lj

## The meta layer (plan 009 P6)
`pg.meta` holds what is computed about a problem rather than in it (thesis el:59: not one of the seven elements). Each
object is built on one data-space decomposition and is a record whose claims say that it is meta (key "meta") and
whether it used the datum (key "used the datum"; a choice made from the datum adds "data-dependent rule", el:524-526).
`pg.meta.TradeOff(dec, T, datum=None)` is the closed-form (sigma, TRM) Pareto curve for any array of gamma (`.at`,
`.values`); `pg.meta.Knee(rule="chord", axes=("trm", "log sigma"), rescale="unit", side="convex",
refine="continuous")` is a declared knee rule (the thesis's r3; `refine="continuous"` maximises the chord distance on
the closed-form curve, `refine=(30, 10.0)` is the thesis's grid rule) and `.find(curve, lo, hi)` a `KneeResult`;
`pg.meta.OperatingPoints(tc, lo, hi, knee=..., factor=10.0)` the knee and its flanks. `pg.meta.PredictiveCheck(dec, d,
a, nu, bins=...)` gives chi^2_pred and the posterior residual with their laws (chi^2_n and a `pg.WeightedChiSquare`)
and p-values as Enclosures, per bin of rho^2 Lam fixed before the data. `pg.meta.Coverage(dec, T, truth, a=, nu= |
gamma=, level=)` is the exact coverage probability at a fixed truth, Phi((z sigma_post - eps) / sigma_alt) -
Phi((-z sigma_post - eps) / sigma_alt) (appQ:311-319), with `.frequency(estimates)` for draws. `pg.meta.Leakage(dec,
T, gamma, target=...)` splits the delivered kernel by `pg.DirectSum` summand (rrm^2 + rcm^2 = trm^2; `.apply(m)` the
delivered property by summand), `pg.meta.VarianceSplit(dec, T, a, nu)` the PLI posterior variance into propagated noise
and prior-weighted mismatch, and `pg.meta.Lane(element, name, values, baseline=)` records a one-at-a-time sensitivity
with the element it changes (prior, noise, MDR, target, realisation, ...; the elements are pg.Problem's). The
data-dependent choice rules take (problem, method, hyperparameter family) and return a `pg.meta.Choice` that states
the family as a named record (key "hyperparameter family") and whether the datum entered (plan 070 P2):
`pg.meta.discrepancy(pb, "least_squares", pg.meta.damping("least_squares", centre=c))` (chi^2 = n; `pb.run(
"least_squares", **ch.options())`), `pg.meta.maximum_evidence(pb, "pli", pg.meta.scales())` ((a, nu)),
`pg.meta.fit_noise(pb, "pli", pg.meta.NoiseFamily(D, groups="s"))` and `pg.meta.knee(pb, "sola",
pg.meta.damping("sola", lo=, hi=))`; `pg.meta.RULES` lists them. Tests: tests/test_meta.py (dense solves, analytic
knees, Monte Carlo of the laws and of the coverage, the ladder), tests/test_meta_choice.py. Background: EXP-030
DESIGN.md G11.

```python
import numpy as np, pygeoinf0 as pg
rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
M, D = pg.Euclidean(30), pg.Euclidean(12, covariance=0.1)
G = pg.Matrix(rng.normal(size=(12, 30)), M, D, name="G")
T = pg.Matrix(rng.normal(size=(1, 30)), M, pg.Euclidean(1), name="T")
d = D(G.A @ rng.normal(size=30) + np.sqrt(0.1) * rng.normal(size=12))
dec = pg.decompose(G, D.standard_gaussian())         # SOLA's decomposition, once
tc = pg.meta.TradeOff(dec, T, datum=d)               # sigma(gamma), TRM(gamma) in closed form
op = pg.meta.OperatingPoints(tc, 1e-4, 1e4, knee=pg.meta.Knee(refine="continuous"))
print(op.gamma, op.sigma, op.trm, op.estimate)
pc = pg.meta.PredictiveCheck(dec, d, a=1.0, nu=1.0, bins=3)   # prior a^2 I on M: S is the model metric
print(pc.chi2, pc.p_value, pc.residual_p_value)
vs = pg.meta.VarianceSplit(dec, T, a=1.0, nu=1.0)
print(vs.noise_share, vs.claims)
```


## Scale: the same code from 10^2 to 10^5 data (plan 005)
A script written for a few hundred data runs unchanged at 10^5: only the routes behind `G @ G.adjoint`, `.inv`,
`solvers.damping_for_misfit`, `condition` and `post.std` change, chosen by size and structure, and every approximate route
says so in the claims (route, tolerance, estimate). Thresholds measured on europa (16 cores, 62 GB):

| regime | data n | Gram / products | solve, alpha | posterior std |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| exact | n <= `settings.dense_max` (10^4) | dense matrix of exact energies (L3 at 10^4: 10.9 GB peak) | Cholesky / eigh | exact |
| matrix-free | n > dense_max | `G G*` stays an operator (`matrix()` refused); products by `settings.engine` | CG stopping at 1 % of a noise std (rms); discrepancy by Lanczos | randomised Nystrom decomposition with a bound per functional, or deflated block CG (below) |

- **Engines** (`settings.engine = "auto"`): above dense_max, segments in 3-D use the spectral engine (tapered NUFFT
  far field plus exact corrections on close segment pairs; needs the optional `finufft`, extra "fast"), with a
  two-resolution estimate of the relative product error (1.5-2x the actual error in the validation); otherwise the
  H-matrix (practical to about 10^4 rays: its exact near field is refused by `settings.memory_gb` beyond). The exact
  corrections grow like n^2 at fixed correlation length: at 10^5 rays of 27 segments (l = 500 km) they exceed
  `settings.memory_gb = 40` and are refused with the reason; `settings.collinear = 0.3` corrects only pairs of different
  rays that meet or are nearly collinear (L3 at 10^5: 2.3e7 pairs, 808 s setup, 15.7 GB; error within the target).
- **Posterior std above dense_max** (`post.std(u)`, probability/scale.py): Var = <C u, u> - w_u^T (W + I)^-1 w_u in
  whitened data coordinates; a randomised Nystrom W_k (one-sided: the std is never overstated by it) with ||W - W_k||
  estimated by probes (probability >= 1 - 1e-10), plus the engine's estimated product error propagated; functionals
  whose bound exceeds `settings.accuracy` (1 %) of their std are recomputed by deflated block CG. When the rank reaches
  about the number of functionals p with ||W - W_k|| still above the tail (every ladder problem at 10^4 and L3 at 10^5),
  all p go to deflated block CG (route by cost). Measured against the exact posterior with the same alpha at 10^4:
  L1, L2, L4 within 1e-12, L3 within 2.5e-5, every functional inside its stated bound. Cost of the CG route: p columns
  per iteration through the engine (L3 at 10^5, p = 200: 91 iterations, 7.4 h at 8 workers), so ask for the
  functionals you need.
- **Forcing a route**: `with pg.settings.using(dense_max=1000): ...` (the matrix-free routes on a small problem, e.g.
  to compare), `pg.settings.engine = "exact" | "hmatrix" | "spectral"`, `pg.settings.collinear = 0.3`,
  `pg.settings.accuracy = 0.1` (decision 3: up to 10 % of the statistical uncertainty when speed matters),
  `post.std(u, exact=True)` (block CG for every functional), `pg.settings.workers` (or `PYGEOINF0_WORKERS`).
- **Not on a fast engine yet**: evaluating a model m(x) at many points is points x segments (direct sums; the global
  tomography sections at 10^4 data took 34 min on 30 workers).
- Demonstration: `examples/global_tomography.py` with `N_DATA` (benchmarks/global_scale.py runs it at scale and adds
  the posterior std; results in plan 005's status).


## Bring your own implementation: the port, plugins and nonlinear drivers (plan 019, 2026-10-30)

pygeoinf0 contains no application code (no ray tracer, no PDE solver). External code connects through the port
(`pygeoinf0.port`; design: experiments/EXP-034-external-implementations/DESIGN.md):

- `pg.port.Coordinates(M, ...)`: the numbers an external code is given. The export must be bounded on M: nodal values
  on H^s with s > d/2 (refused on L2, where point values do not exist), cell averages on L2, any functionals given by
  measures, or a Euclidean space's own numbers. The default import is the least-norm element with given numbers.
- `pg.port.External(M, D, g, coords=..., dom=...)`: a map given by your callable g on numbers, as an ordinary pg.Map with a
  declared domain; failures inside dom raise pg.ExternalError naming your code.
- Derivatives: `jvp=`/`vjp=` (AD tools, adjoint codes), `jacobian=`, or `measures=` (the derivative of each datum as
  a measure, e.g. a ray path). `G.derivative(theta)` is a LinearOperator whose adjoint uses M's and D's inner
  products. The derivative of a scalar map is a `pg.Functional` (`pg.Functional.from_operator(DJ)`, an element of
  `M.dual()`); `pg.gradient` is its Riesz representer, which depends on M's inner product (a tool's "gradient" g is
  that of its coordinates' dot product: `C.functional(g)` is the functional it stands for).
- `pg.port.taylor_test(G, theta)`: Taylor rates and the adjoint identity, as checked claims; kinks are reported.
- `pg.optimisers.gauss_newton(G, d, theta, alpha=)` (Levenberg-Marquardt, steps in the data space) and `pg.optimisers.lbfgs(J, theta,
  alpha=)` (in M's inner product): the core's two nonlinear drivers. Robust losses, sampling and the rest are plugins.
- Plugins (`pg.port.plugins`): separate packages that register a `pg.port.plugins.Plugin`, explicitly or through the entry
  point group `pygeoinf0.plugins` (`pg.port.plugins.available()` lists installed ones without importing them). Their
  claims are asserted or checked and carry the plugin's name and version. In this workspace they live in plugins/
  (each its own Git repository, private on GitHub; plugins/README.md has the pins); the first is pygeoinf0-obspy
  (TauP ray paths). The two TauP examples, examples/global_tomography.py and moon_tomography.py, take their ray code
  from that plugin (since 2026-10-30, the author's decision; results bit-identical to their former own ray code,
  checked on europa at N_DATA 600 and 1000).

```python
import numpy as np, pygeoinf0 as pg
I, x = pg.Interval("[0, 1]"), np.array([0.2, 0.5, 0.8])
b = np.array([0.3, 1.0, 0.5])
for s in (1, 2):
    M = pg.Sobolev(I, s, length_scale=0.2)
    C = pg.port.Coordinates.nodal(M, x)                       # u -> (u(0.2), u(0.5), u(0.8)): bounded on H^s, s > 1/2
    G = pg.port.External(M, pg.Euclidean(3), lambda v: v ** 2, coords=(C, None), jacobian=lambda v: np.diag(2 * v))
    J = pg.port.External(M, pg.Euclidean(1), lambda v: np.array([0.5 * np.sum((v ** 2 - b) ** 2)]), coords=(C, None),
                    jacobian=lambda v: ((v ** 2 - b) * 2 * v)[None, :])
    m0 = M(lambda t: 0.5 + 0 * t)
    print(pg.port.taylor_test(G, m0).rates)                   # ~2 in every direction
    grad = pg.gradient(J, m0)                            # differs between H^1 and H^2; the derivative does not
    fit = pg.optimisers.gauss_newton(G, pg.Euclidean(3)(b), m0, alpha=1e-2)
    print(s, fit.converged, fit.value)
```

## A bank of functions: pygeoinf0.functions (plan 021, 2026-10-30)

An optional subpackage (`import pygeoinf0` does not load it) of 40 families on 175 (family, domain kind) entries:
bumps and Gaussians, SOLA targets and windows, tapers, Wendland/Wu/Buhmann kernels, indicators, polynomials and
spherical harmonics, Laplacian eigenfunctions, finite-element-like functions, test fields, and functions on curves,
cylinders and tori. `FUNCTIONS.md` is the catalogue, generated from the registry (`python -m pygeoinf0.functions`).
Design: experiments/EXP-037-function-bank/DESIGN.md.

A bank function is a function, not an element: a vectorised callable of Cartesian coordinates that carries its domain,
parameters, support, regularity and closed forms (integral, squared L2 norm, maximum) as claims with their status.
`M(f)` works as for any callable; `f.element(M)` first checks the claims against M (L2 and Lp: bounded; C(K):
continuous; H^s: the regularity's Sobolev bound and the boundary conditions of M's construction) and refuses with the
reason otherwise. For SOLA, `f.measure(M.measure)` is f dmu on the support, so `M.riesz([f.measure(M.measure)])` is
the representer of m -> int f m dmu; `f.normalised(against=mu)` gives unit mass against a weighted measure. Every
entry passes one contract test (vectorisation, support, closed forms against an independent quadrature, gradient,
membership, claims); radial functions declare their kink radii (`Support.breaks`), where that quadrature is split.

On balls and shells (plan 035 P2) `f.fit_breaks(D)` gives natural-coordinate breaks from a support ball B(c, rho):
r = d -+ rho, the cap theta_c -+ delta and its longitudes (delta = asin(rho / d)), with a least angular degree 2 pi /
delta and the support's box (`realisations.backends.NaturalBreaks`). `M(f)` and densities w dx on a whole layer use
them, so narrow and off-axis bumps are fitted (the tensor fit of a 3 degree bump off the axis was 0 with error 0
before). A fit that is zero for a bank function whose closed norm is positive and whose support meets the domain raises
`RealisationError`. The radial x harmonic fit starts at the least degree, refuses one above its cap (127), and is
checked a posteriori on the support box: an error above its own estimate replaces it (a warning), one above 5 % of the
norm is refused. A `ZeroFunctional` from realised kernels names their realisation.

```python
import numpy as np, pygeoinf0 as pg, pygeoinf0.functions as F
B = pg.Ball(1.0)
f = F.on(B).bump(centre=(0, 0, 0.4), radius=0.3)            # the families defined on B's kind, bound to B
f.claims                                                    # support, regularity (C-infinity), closed forms
try:
    F.on(B).indicator(radius=0.3).element(pg.Sobolev(B, 1, length_scale=0.2))
except pg.Refused as e:                                     # refused: it jumps, so it is in H^s only for s < 1/2
    print(e)
I = pg.Interval("[0, 1]")
M = pg.Sobolev(I, 1, length_scale=0.1)
t = F.on(I).bump(centre=0.5, radius=0.1).normalised()                       # an averaging kernel
T = pg.Functionals(M.riesz([t.measure(M.measure)]))                         # the target functional m -> int t m
F.catalogue()                                                               # [(family, kinds, summary)]
```

An averaging kernel is `f.normalised()`, for any function of the bank that can be read as a localized average; there is no
separate version of a family for it. `f.normalised(against=mu)` has int f dmu = 1. It warns (`AveragingWarning`) when f changes
sign or its integral is nearly zero, and it is refused when the integral is 0.

examples/function_bank_sola.py runs SOLA with four target shapes (box, smooth box, Gaussian, bump) on one data set,
and a target normalised against L2(r^2 dr).

## Complex-valued spaces (plan 020 P3, 2026-10-30)

A complex space is a real Hilbert space with a complex structure J (J x = i x), so adjoints, least squares, Bayesian
updates and the port's checks work on it unchanged (design: experiments/EXP-035-mineral-exploration/COMPLEX.md). The
complex inner product is linear in the first argument and conjugate-linear in the second; the real one is its real
part.

- `pg.Euclidean(n, field="C", weights= | metric= | covariance=)`: C^n with native complex storage; `X.cinner`, `X.J`
  (plan 068 P1: one Euclidean class and one metric code for R^n and C^n; a covariance is solved with, not inverted).
- `pg.Complexified(H)`: the complexification H (+) H of any real Hilbert space (complex formulas, parts, `lift(A)`).
  For a cell-realised L2 it stores one complex array of cell values; `pg.port.Coordinates.cells` then exports C^n.
- `pg.ComplexMatrix(A, X, Y)`: complex-linear operators between complex Euclidean spaces (adjoint W_X^-1 A^H W_Y).
- `pygeoinf0.probability.circular.circular(X, S, m)`: circular complex Gaussians CN(m, S) of the coordinates (`X.standard_gaussian()` on C^n: CN(0, W^-1)). Claim "circular" (theorem), kept
  through complex-linear push-forwards and conditioning (derived).
- `pg.port.complex_linearity(A)`: checks A J = J A as a claim. `pg.port.External` takes complex jvp/vjp (a vjp returns J^H w);
  `pg.port.taylor_test` uses complex directions on complex numbers.

examples/cole_cole_cells.py: the complex conductivity of a chargeable body (Cole-Cole) on a cell mesh, from a circular
prior and complex data.

## Functionals by weighted measures and lazy elements (plan 022, 2026-10-30/31)

A functional F(m) = int m w dmu (pg.LebesgueMeasure(S).with_density(w), or a bank function's f.measure()) is defined
by its integral. Evaluating it does no energy work. Representers, Gram matrices and norms are built on the first
operation that needs them, once (design: experiments/EXP-038-weighted-energies).

- **Realised weights.** A weight is realised on its own set by one adaptive rule that every consumer shares
  (evaluation, moments, energies). Its error indicator comes from the weight itself, with a rate test that tells smooth
  weights from jumps and kinks. Narrow kernels keep their mass: a bump of radius 0.1 on [0, 1] integrates to 1 within
  1e-12 in L2 and H^1, with no warnings for interval densities.
- **Energies in 2-D.** Smooth weights (C^inf bumps) on disks and boxes take a spectral route with a far/near split: a
  coarse lattice for the long range, local lattices at each weight's own resolution for the short range, and shared
  spectra for translated copies. Weights whose indicator does not settle (jumps, kinks) go to the exact geometric
  routes. The route used and its estimate are in the claims and in M.backend.engine.grid.report.
  `pg.settings.weighted_grid = "off"` forces the exact routes.
- **Lazy elements.** Bank functions, coordinates (M.x) and constants evaluate and plot from their formula. Their
  linear combinations keep the terms: values, inner products, integrals and functionals are taken term by term, and
  the merged realisation is built only when needed (norms, products, solvers). Bank elements pass their support edges
  and kinks to the fit, so a narrow bump is not realised as zero.

```python
import numpy as np, pygeoinf0 as pg, pygeoinf0.functions as F
B = pg.Box((0, 1), (0, 1))
M = pg.Sobolev(B, 1, length_scale=0.1)
centres = [(x, y) for x in np.linspace(0.15, 0.85, 5) for y in np.linspace(0.15, 0.85, 5)]
mus = [F.on(B).bump(centre=c, radius=0.1).measure() for c in centres]
T = pg.Functionals(M, M.riesz(mus))                       # 25 functionals; nothing assembled yet
y = T(M(lambda x, y: np.sin(2 * np.pi * x) * np.cos(2 * np.pi * y)))   # values by integration
G = T.gram()                                              # energies now: split-spectral route
```

Timings for this 25-bump case on europa: Gram in 0.32 s, 1.4e-13 of max |G| from an independent Hankel reference.
Before plan 022 it took 92 s, with 1 % errors and warnings up to 100 %. 25 bumps of radius 0.02: 0.24 s, 1.5e-15.
400 bumps of radius 0.04: 4.1 s, 5.9e-15. The sum of 100 bumps, evaluated and plotted: 0.2 s (36.8 s before).

## Classic discretisation by a basis (plan 025, 2026-10-31; two roles since plan 068 P6)

A finite subspace has two roles, and two objects. `V = pg.Span(basis, space=M)` (convex/subspaces.py) is the span of N
given functions of a Hilbert function space M as a set: a closed subspace of M (a state of kind set, e.g. a grid taken as
the model space) with its Gram (mass) matrix `V.gram`, the coefficient space `V.coefficient_space = Euclidean(N,
metric=G)` (coefficient norms are norms in M), the synthesis `V.synthesis` S: c -> sum c_i phi_i and its adjoint
`V.analysis` S* = G^-1 (<phi_i, .>), so `V.project` = S S* is the orthogonal projection; `V.loads(f)`,
`V.coefficients(f)`, `V.element(c)`, `V.solve(b)`. Independence is checked once, scale-free, by a factor of the
correlation matrix (Cholesky, or a sparse LU for many local functions); dependent generators span a smaller space, with
least-norm coefficients, and the maps that need a basis refuse with the numerical rank.

`D = pg.Discretisation(V)` (operators/galerkin.py) is the approximation role: V stands for M in a problem that stays
posed on M (a space is never identified with a truncation), and every number of D is a number of M on the span. Its
claims say what it approximates and how well, in the norm of M and never in coefficient coordinates: "uniform
approximation" is a theorem, FALSE whenever V is smaller than M (a unit element orthogonal to V exists);
"approximation" over a class is UNKNOWN until the caller states it, `D.state(e, over, how)` (ASSERTED); `D.check(fs)`
gives one Enclosure of ||f - P f||_M per element, widened by the realisation errors, and records a CHECKED claim about
those elements only (a check never promotes the claim about a class). Operators: `D.matrix(T)` = T o S for T into R^n,
`D.pairings(measures)` the data x basis matrix of points, rays and boxes, `D.galerkin(A, test)` = (<psi_j, A phi_i>),
`D.operator(A, test)` = G'^-1 K in coefficients (exact on the span; nothing is claimed about ||A - P' A P||),
`D.gram_in(N)` the Gram of the same functions in another space. Bases come from the bank: `F.bases.hats`, `bsplines`,
`pixels`, `legendre`, `fourier`, `spherical_harmonics`, `zernike`.

On boxes the default realisation is `"local"` (the author, 2026-10-31; `realisation="tensor"` gives the previous
default): each function with a stated support box is fitted on that box only, a translated copy of a bank function is
the earlier fit shifted (checked at sample points), sums keep their patches, Grams are sparse over overlapping pairs, translates share one coefficient array and one Gram
block per offset (like an element matrix), and `D.pairings` is exact for the polynomial pieces (rays split at the
breaks; pixels give exact path lengths). `M(f, support=box)` states a support box for your own function (f times the
box's indicator on every realisation). Classic H^1 (FEM) norms are those of the form space
`pg.SobolevForm(B, 1, length_scale=l).space()` (int u v + l^2 grad u . grad v, natural Neumann; Dirichlet faces with
`dirichlet=`): there the hat Gram is the FEM matrix. The default `pg.Sobolev(B, 1, length_scale=l)` is the restriction
space, whose norm of two formulas needs an extension problem and is refused (NormUnavailable, naming the form space).

```python
import numpy as np, pygeoinf0 as pg, pygeoinf0.functions as F
B = pg.Box((0, 1), (0, 1))
V = pg.SobolevForm(B, 1, length_scale=0.1).space(realisation="local")
D = pg.Discretisation(pg.Span(F.bases.hats(B, 41, boundary=True), space=V))   # 1681 hats: G = M x M + l^2 (K x M + M x K)
rays = [pg.Segment((0.1, 0.2), (0.9, 0.7)), pg.Segment((0.5, 0.05), (0.4, 0.95))]
E = D.pairings(rays)                                             # sparse, exact; E.claims
W = D.subspace
c = np.linalg.solve(E.A.toarray().T @ E.A.toarray() + 1e-3 * W.gram, E.A.toarray().T @ np.ones(2))
m = W.element(c); m.norm(); W.coefficient_space.norm(W.coefficient_space(c))   # equal
P = pg.Span(F.bases.pixels(B, 64), space=pg.L2(B, realisation="local"))     # the classic pixel basis, as a set
P.coefficients(P.space(lambda x, y: x * y))                     # cell averages
S = pg.Sphere()
Y = pg.Discretisation(pg.Span(F.bases.spherical_harmonics(S, 8), space=pg.L2(S)))  # 81 harmonics: G = I
Y.check(pg.L2(S)(lambda x, y, z: np.exp(z)))                    # ||f - P f|| per element, CHECKED
```

Measured (europa, EXP-039; tests/test_local_realisation.py, tests/test_discretisation.py): 100 bumps, L2: the merged
realisation of their sum 25.6 s -> 0.003 s, its norm 0.31 s -> 0.12 s, equal to 1.6e-15; 1521 hats in L2: 0.9 s,
the Gram equal to the Kronecker P1 mass matrix to 2e-15; rays x pixels and rays x hats exact to 1e-16 against
independent references, 10^4 rays x 1521 hats 0.95 s. One problem two ways (p6_compare.py: 200 rays, Tikhonov in the
form space, alpha from the continuum route): the continuum solution 30 s; Galerkin with 121 .. 6561 hats 0.2 .. 11 s,
converging to it at the rate h^(1/2) that the representers' regularity (H^(3/2 - eps)) allows (relative distance
22 %, 16 %, 11 %, 7.7 %).
